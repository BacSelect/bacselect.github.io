The idea

One number in.
A defensible panel out.

Benchmarking a tool should not require weeks of manually deciding which bacterial genomes are “representative”. BacSelect turns that selection problem into a reproducible resource.

01

Choose your scale

Pick a tractable panel size for your compute budget or analysis. The same release and N always return the same panel.

02

Span genome architecture

Selection is driven by sequence-derived structural features rather than species popularity, pathogen status or biological importance.

03

Download and reproduce

Every released panel will carry accessions, structural features, selection provenance, checksums and an immutable release identifier.

Selection principle

Not random.
Not hand-picked.

BacSelect is designed around identity-blind, deterministic, diversity-seeking selection.

The selector may use species groupings to prevent abundant species from dominating the source universe, but it does not choose genomes because their names are familiar, clinically important or convenient.

The aim is specific: build compact panels that span observed bacterial genome architecture. BacSelect will not claim to be an unbiased sample of bacterial life or a universal measure of biological diversity.

Where this came from
“I need a manageable bacterial genome set. Which genomes do I choose without quietly choosing the answer first?”

BacSelect grew out of the source-genome selection problem in Project Finch, where more than 55,000 complete bacterial assemblies had to be reduced to a small benchmark panel before assembler performance was observed.
